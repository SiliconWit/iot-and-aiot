Many smallholder farmers still water by hand and check their crops by eye for pests, disease and nutrient problems. The decisions depend on the person, water is wasted, and yields vary from season to season. Crop health depends on moisture, temperature, humidity, nutrients, disease pressure and maturity acting together. When each is watched on its own, the grower reacts to symptoms, not to causes.

Tools exist for parts of the problem. IoT irrigation saves water, and small image models can detect leaf disease with over 90% accuracy. Few systems do both.

## What we did

We designed one low-cost system for amaranth that waters only when the soil needs it and warns about pests, disease, nutrient status and the best time to harvest.

Sensors measure soil moisture, air temperature and humidity, water flow and soil nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium (NPK). A camera module takes leaf images. An ESP32 microcontroller controls the field.

The irrigation decision runs on the ESP32, so watering continues without internet. When soil moisture falls below a set threshold, relays start the pump and open the solenoid valves. A flow sensor records the water applied, and the valves close once the moisture recovers.

Readings go over Wi-Fi to the SiliconWit.io cloud. Machine learning models there are meant to detect pests and disease, predict nutrient status and estimate harvest time from the time-series data. We tested the control circuit in a Proteus simulation and designed a custom PCB for the prototype.

## What we found

- The Proteus simulation confirmed automatic watering: the pump started when soil moisture fell below the threshold.
- Real-world installation and data collection are planned for the fourth quarter.

## Why it matters

A farmer gets watering that follows the soil, not a fixed routine, and it does not stop when the network does. The same single controller also feeds the cloud models that give early warnings. If field tests confirm the design, the farmer would rely less on guesswork for both watering and crop checks.

## Limits

This summary is written from the conference abstract. The only result so far is a circuit simulation. There are no field measurements of water saved, and the pest, disease, nutrient and harvest models have not yet been trained on local data. The next step is to train an image model on local amaranth leaves and test whether colour images can estimate nutrient status and detect disease in real field conditions.
