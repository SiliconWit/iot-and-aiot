A small workshop, a training lab or a student who needs to make parts usually has to buy two machines. A CNC machine removes material from a block to reach the final shape. A 3D printer adds material layer by layer from a digital file. On the market these exist as separate products, so the buyer pays for two machines.

## What we did

We set out to develop one computer numerically controlled machine that handles both subtractive and additive tasks at low cost.

After a review of earlier work, we developed the software design and the architecture under which the machine operates. We designed the power supply. We selected a suitable microcontroller and motor drivers and designed their circuits in Proteus to form the control block. We did the mechanical design in Autodesk Inventor, which gave the machine block.

The project was planned to be completed over two semesters, following a work plan.

## What we found

- The work produced a software design and architecture, a power supply design, a control block built around the selected microcontroller and motor drivers, and a mechanical design of the machine.
- The abstract reports the design stage. It does not report a built and tested machine or any measured cutting or printing performance.

## Why it matters

The project names three uses for a machine that can both cut and print. It could improve the machines used in modern manufacturing and production. As a learning kit, it would let students learn both CNC machining and 3D printing on one machine. And it would allow components to be made from different materials, all in one machine.

## Limits

This summary is written from the project abstract. It describes the design work and the plan, not a finished machine. There are no results on accuracy, speed, surface finish, print quality or the final cost. How the machine switches between cutting and printing, and how it handles the different loads of the two processes, is not described in the abstract.
